Everyone has a theory about the tube. Mine is that it is the only room in
London where three hundred strangers agree, without discussion, to stand in
complete silence. It is a cathedral with a delay board.

<TubeMap />

The map is a lie, and a beautiful one. It straightens every curve, equalises
every distance, and turns a twenty-minute walk into two neat dots. Harry Beck
did that in 1931 and nobody has dared touch it since. Hover the lines above —
each one is a different country with its own rules.

<PlatformBoard />

The departure board is the only honest screen in the city. It does not
recommend. It does not upsell. It says a train is in one minute, and the
train is in one minute, and that is the entire contract. When it says
"check front of train", you check the front of the train.

<TrainRide />

Scroll on and the train goes. That is the whole simulation — no score, no
finish line, just the quiet satisfaction of passing Tottenham Court Road at
a reasonable pace. This is what the tube feels like from the inside: a
window, a rumble, and a list of places you are nearly at.

<TubeAd />

And when you get off, mind the gap. Not because of the poster. Because the
platform and the train have never quite agreed on where the floor is.

— J.
